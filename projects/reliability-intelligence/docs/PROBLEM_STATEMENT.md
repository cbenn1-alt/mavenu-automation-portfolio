# V2 — narrow problem statement (CANDIDATE; not approved)

## Intended decision

Should a cooperative or sugar-processing plant sponsor a small, **read-only maintenance review workflow** for a single identified asset family, or does its current maintenance/ERP/reporting stack already solve that problem?

## Buyer, user and accountable roles

| Role | Proposed responsibility | Verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Economic buyer | Plant operations or maintenance leadership; approves cost | Unconfirmed |
| Primary user | Maintenance planner or reliability engineer; reviews candidate cases | Unconfirmed |
| Sponsor / process owner | Accepts problem, operational baseline and workflow | Not assigned |
| IS&T / security | Checks architecture, access rights and vendor roadmap | Not assigned |
| Maintenance/safety SME | Reviews domain exceptions and hazardous misinterpretations | Not assigned |

## Hypothesis, not diagnosis

For one approved asset family, planners **may** spend avoidable time reconciling fragmented historical work orders, repeat-failure information, repair expense and downtime evidence. They might benefit from source-traceable, human-reviewed candidate cases. This has **not** been verified with Minn-Dak staff or records. Do not assert that Minn-Dak has deficient software or unreliable operations.

## In-scope first evaluation

A synthetic-only, local review exercise comparing manual and assisted preparation for the same pre-registered maintenance cases. No automatic final priorities, work-order creation, budget allocation, plant commands, or safety-critical recommendations. Do not collect customer data to prepare the first demonstration.

## Exclusions / disqualifying conditions

- Existing CMMS, Dynamics 365, Levridge, Power BI or partner tooling already addresses the workflow at lower net cost.
- No credible business sponsor, missing authorized data, incompatible source semantics, no measurable baseline, or unclear failure consequence.
- Unbounded integration/security requirements, uncontrolled review outputs, safety concerns or no economic value after full implementation and support cost.

## Exit decision

Customer/owner must decide **build / configure / buy / stop**, document one cohort and owner, agree the baseline and scorecard, and sign written scope before engineering advances beyond synthetic requirements prototyping. A committed engagement with Minn-Dak does not exist.
